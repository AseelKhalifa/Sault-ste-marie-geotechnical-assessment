Geological, geotechnical, and environmental assessment of hypothetical nuclear energy infrastructure in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, focusing on foundation engineering, infrastructure resilience, and sustainable development.
# Building Power on Solid Ground
### Geotechnical and Environmental Assessment of Nuclear Energy Infrastructure | Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario

## Project Overview

This research report investigates the geological, geotechnical, and environmental feasibility considerations associated with the hypothetical development of small modular reactor (SMR) infrastructure in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario.

The study applies civil engineering principles to evaluate subsurface conditions, foundation alternatives, environmental risks, and long-term infrastructure resilience.

## Scope of Research

- Regional geology and subsurface characterization
- Foundation engineering and structural stability
- Seismic, flooding, and erosion hazards
- Hydrological and hydrogeological assessment
- Environmental impact and ecological sustainability
- Sustainable stormwater management
- Proposed GIS-based site suitability assessment
- Climate adaptation and infrastructure life-cycle management
- Engineering risk assessment and regulatory considerations

## Engineering Competencies

- Geotechnical engineering
- Geological interpretation
- Infrastructure planning
- Environmental risk assessment
- Foundation system evaluation
- Sustainable engineering
- Technical research and report writing
- GIS applications in civil engineering

## Research Methodology

The assessment incorporates literature-based research, conceptual engineering models, comparative foundation analysis, and qualitative risk assessment to establish recommendations for future site-specific investigations.

## Project Deliverable

The complete research report includes technical discussions, geological illustrations, engineering diagrams, comparative tables, and recommendations for future infrastructure development.
